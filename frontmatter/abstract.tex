\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A state-space mechatronic control system was designed and implemented for the regulation of longitudinal speed and steering angle in a 1:10 scale ground vehicle. The coupled dynamics of traction and steering were modeled analytically and experimentally, deriving a matrix state-space representation to formally evaluate controllability and observability properties. A comprehensive mechatronic architecture was integrated, comprising speed and angular position sensors, electronic power drivers, a steering servo actuator, a direct-current traction motor, and a digital processing unit for real-time periodic execution of the state-feedback control law. The system performance was quantitatively evaluated under step reference changes, load disturbances, and power supply voltage drops on controlled surfaces, successfully reducing steady-state speed error and stabilizing transient orientation response within the specified design criteria.

\vspace{1.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} 1:10 scale vehicle, mechatronic integration, speed regulation, state-space control, steering regulation, vehicular dynamics.
